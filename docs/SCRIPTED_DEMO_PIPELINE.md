# Scripted Demo Pipeline — Plan and Feasibility Audit

**Goal:** replace the hand-recorded instructional videos on gfpricechecker.github.io with
videos and stills generated by a script the app plays against itself, so that a new build
regenerates the whole media set instead of requiring a re-shoot.

**Status:** Stages 1-6 done; Stage 7 not started. This document is the grounding for the
work and records the audit that sized it.

**Context:** this effort sits ahead of the App Store release that ends GFPriceChecker's
year-long beta. The media set is part of that release, and the point of scripting it is that
it stops being a thing that rots between builds.

---

## 1. Why this, and where the concept comes from

The pattern is proven in a sibling project, TrendFit (`../TrendFitApp_App`), which generates
every walkthrough video and tile still on its website from scripts the app plays in the
Simulator with no hand driving it. Its tooling is `Tools/Demo/` and its in-app half is
`Features/Dev/Demo/`; `Tools/Demo/README.md` there is 529 lines of measured findings and is
the single most valuable thing to port.

The core idea is one sentence: **a script drives the app's own ViewModels, so every gesture is
a `withAnimation` or an eased ramp, and the take is identical every run.** A mouse-driven
recording jitters and drifts; a scripted one does not, and can be regenerated after any UI
change by re-running a shell command.

What that buys here specifically:

- A UI change means re-running `record.sh`, not re-shooting a 90-second narrated tutorial.
- The still images on the site are cut from the *same* script as the videos, so a tile and its
  video cannot drift apart.
- Status bar, appearance, and data state are all deterministic — no red recording pill, no
  9:41-versus-11:04, no "whatever was in the store that day."

### What we produce today, for contrast

| | GFPriceChecker (today) | TrendFit (scripted) |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution | 1920×1080 landscape | 1170×2544 portrait |
| Duration | 60–92 s | 19–27 s |
| Audio | yes | none (autoplay loops) |
| Captions | burned-in overlays | none |
| Source | physical iPhone, hand-driven | Simulator, scripted |

Files: `assets/video/{price-tracking,receipt-management,reporting-exporting}.mp4` in the
website repo, embedded from `features.html`.

These are *different deliverables*, and that difference is deliberate on both sides — ours are
narrated tutorials, TrendFit's are silent feature loops. Scripting the capture does not mean
adopting TrendFit's format. It means the capture half becomes reproducible; the landscape
frame, captions and voice-over stay, as a compositing stage TrendFit does not have.

## 2. Audit findings

Audited 2026-09-13 against `main` at 691065a. Four blockers, in the order they bite.

### Blocker 1 — the capture flows cannot run in the Simulator at all — **fixed, Stage 1**

`Views/CaptureFlow/DocumentScannerView.swift` wraps `VNDocumentCameraViewController`
(VisionKit). In the Simulator that controller presents a sheet that **never returns an
image**, and there was no guard of any kind — `grep -rn isSupported` returned nothing.

Note what the guard cannot be. `VNDocumentCameraViewController.isSupported` reports **`true`**
in the Simulator (measured, iOS 26 / Xcode 26.1.1). The first attempt at this fix keyed off
`isSupported`, shipped 32 green tests, and changed nothing the user could see, because the
guard never fired. The signal is `targetEnvironment(simulator)`; `isSupported` is consulted
only on a device.

Three call sites depend on it:

- `Views/CaptureFlow/ProductCaptureFlowView.swift:128`
- `Views/CaptureFlow/ReceiptCaptureView.swift:110`
- `Views/CaptureFlow/ProductImageCaptureView.swift:77`

Two of the three website videos — price-tracking and receipt-management — are *about* those
flows. This is almost certainly why the existing videos were shot on a physical device.

**This is the first thing to fix, and it is worth fixing regardless of video**: right now the
app's most complex flow cannot be exercised in the Simulator by anyone, including during
ordinary development.

Resolved in Stage 1 — see §5. The dispatch went *inside* `DocumentScannerView` rather than
behind each of the three call sites, so none of them changed and device behaviour is
untouched.

### Blocker 2 — there is no ViewModel for a script to drive — **fixed for the capture flow (Stage 3), receipt verification (#90) and export (#91)**

`ViewModels/` held one file, `ReportViewModel.swift`. It is `@Observable` but it is a
stateless calculator — `selectedYear` plus pure functions over `[ProductPair]`
(`calculateTotalDifferential`, `groupByCategory`, `monthlySummary`, …). It owns no screen
state, so there is nothing for a director to register and drive.

The actual flow state is view-local `@State`:

| View | Lines | `@State` | What is trapped there |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ProductCaptureFlowView.swift` | 986 | ~35 | `private enum CaptureStep` (8 cases), plus `handleScannedImage`, `handleRectangleSelection`, `parseSizeFromOCR`, `parseUnitPriceFromOCR`, `saveProductPair` as private view methods |
| `ReceiptCaptureView.swift` | — | 17 | its own `ReceiptStep` enum, product selection set, verification index |
| `ExportView.swift` | — | 18 | format, date-range preset, custom range, paper size, generation state |

TrendFit's scripts work because `DemoScripts` calls methods on ViewModels registered through a
one-line `.demoRegistered(viewModel)` hook. Here there is nothing registrable.

Note the second consequence: the capture flow had **zero test coverage** for exactly the same
reason. The suite (~894 lines across `GFPriceCheckerTests`) covered services and reporting, not
the 986-line view holding the app's most intricate logic.

`ProductCaptureFlowView` is done — see §5 Stage 3. `ReceiptCaptureView` is done too (#90):
`ReceiptCaptureViewModel` owns `ReceiptStep` and the 17 properties, takes its OCR and image
storage through `Dependencies` and its `ModelContext` as a parameter to `save(into:)`, and
`ReceiptCaptureViewModelTests` runs a whole verification against canned OCR text. The view
keeps its `@Query` of unverified pairs and hands it to `unverifiedProducts`. `ExportView` is
done as well (#91): `ExportViewModel` owns the format, range and PDF choices, the generation
state and the unverified-products warning, takes CSV and PDF generation and the clock through
`Dependencies`, and `ExportViewModelTests` drives exports through `export()` and
`confirmUnverifiedExport()`. The three export buttons share that one path, since all three
always ended in the share sheet. The extraction also fixed a crash: a custom range picked end
before start built a reversed `ClosedRange`, which traps.

### Blocker 3 — navigation is not addressable — **fixed, Stage 4**

- `ContentView.swift` — `TabView` with **no `selection:` binding**. A director cannot change tabs.
- `CaptureTabView.swift` — opens the flow via `@State private var showingCaptureFlow` and
  `.sheet(isPresented:)`.
- `ProductPairListView.swift` — `NavigationLink(destination:)`, value-less, no path binding.

Nothing in the app can be navigated programmatically.

Resolved in Stage 4 — see §5. All three, plus the Library's two Add sheets and the Reports
tab's Export and Settings sheets, now read their state from one `AppRouter`.

### Blocker 4 — no seed data

There is no sample-data infrastructure. The only `inMemory: true` containers are empty
`#Preview` stubs. Every video needs a populated library: pairs across categories and months,
verified and unverified, with receipts attached.

**But this one is nearly solved already — see §3.2.**

## 3. What we already have going for us

### 3.1 The launch overlay is already a curtain

`GFPriceCheckerApp.swift` holds `LaunchScreenView` for 1.5 s and then fades it over 0.6 s.
That is structurally TrendFit's `DemoCurtainView`, at coincidentally the same 1.5 s
`curtainSeconds` — and it exists to solve the same problem, which is that
`simctl io recordVideo` drops frames through the launch burst.

One caveat to carry forward: `LaunchScreenView` is white plus a 0.1-opacity blue/green
gradient, a logo and a tagline. It is *light* but not *flat*, and TrendFit's
`launch_frame.py` finds the cut by looking for the frames with nothing drawn on them
(`FLAT = 12`, measured as ~5 for an empty frame against 18+ once the app draws). The detector
will need either a flat demo-only curtain or a per-app constant. Do not assume it ports
untouched.

### 3.2 `DataBackupService` is the seeding mechanism, already written

This is the biggest find of the audit. `Services/DataBackupService.swift` already provides
the entire seed path:

- `exportBackup(modelContext:) async throws -> Data` — the whole store as versioned JSON
  (`BackupData`: products, receipts, settings, with images Base64-inlined).
- `importBackup(data:modelContext:strategy:)` with `ImportStrategy.replace`.
- `clearAllData(modelContext:)`.

That maps one-to-one onto TrendFit's Health-export → `demo-dataset.json` → seeded store
pipeline, except we do not have to write the extractor: it ships in the app. `clearAllData` +
`.replace` also gives us TrendFit's hard-won "wipe before re-seeding, always" rule for free.

**And we have real data.** A year of genuine capture data exists on a real device from daily
use. A backup exported from it is a far better demo dataset than anything hand-fabricated —
real product names, real price differentials, real category spread, real receipt photos. This
is the same advantage TrendFit gets from using a real Health export, and for the same reason:
**a fabricated dataset shows the app working on data that cannot argue back, and it looks it.**

### 3.3 The scrub is mandatory, not optional

A real backup carries real PII, and these videos get published on a public website. Before a
real export can seed anything:

- `SettingsBackup` carries `userName`, `accountantEmail` and **`taxID`** verbatim.
- `ReceiptBackup.receiptImageData` is a Base64 photograph of an actual receipt — store,
  date, card digits, unrelated purchases, all of it.
- Product names and prices are real, which is *fine and desirable*, but store names may
  want normalizing.

So the pipeline needs a scrubbing stage between export and seed, and its output — the scrubbed
dataset — is what gets committed or kept outside the repo. TrendFit keeps its working files in
`~/TrendFitDemoData/` outside the repo and gitignores them anyway as a belt-and-braces; do the
same here. **A raw backup must never be committed.**

Treat receipt images as the hardest part: they cannot be scrubbed by editing JSON fields,
because the PII is *in the photograph*. Either substitute staged receipt photos, or restrict
the videos to flows that do not display a receipt image full-screen.

**Resolved, and neither of those.** `scrub_backup.py` *draws* each substitute from the data
that survives the scrub — the GF products that receipt verifies, their prices, the purchase
date, the normalized chain name — reusing the layout `SampleScanImages` measured its way to
in Stage 1 (single-space rows; see Stage 1's note — that layout is no longer forced, just not
yet changed). `extractedOCRText` is regenerated from the same lines, and `totalAmount` is
recomputed from the items, so the receipt on screen adds up to the total beside it. No staging
session, and the videos can show a receipt full-screen. Also note `extractedOCRText`, which
§3.3 originally missed: it is the text read off the real photograph, so it carries the same
information in plain sight and needs the same treatment.

## 4. What ports from TrendFit, in three tiers

**Tier 1 — near-verbatim (the host half).** `Tools/Demo/record.sh`, `launch_frame.py`,
`stills.sh`. ~400 lines of app-agnostic mechanics: status-bar override, `simctl io
recordVideo`, frame-based launch detection, `tpad` tail reconstruction, ffmpeg transcode. Only
the bundle ID, the env-var prefix and the working directory are project-specific.
`launch_frame.py` needs the flatness caveat in §3.1 addressed.

**Tier 2 — ports as a shape, not as code.** `DemoDirector`, `DemoCurtainView`,
`DemoPrewarmView`, and the release-side `#if DEBUG` hooks. The mechanism transfers: a singleton
armed from an env var in `App.init()`, release views carrying one-line hooks whose `#else`
bodies are `self`, a done-file the shell polls, a still-file marking the frame a tile is cut
from, an `async push` that holds a settle interval. The contents are per-app.

**Tier 3 — does not port.** TrendFit's `build_demo_dataset.py`, `preflight_dataset.py`,
`DemoDataImporter` and `notification_take.sh` are all HealthKit-specific. Our equivalent is
§3.2, and it is less work.

**Tier 0 — port first: the knowledge.** `Tools/Demo/README.md`. The findings that cost real
time there are the non-obvious ones, and they will apply here unchanged:

- `simctl io recordVideo` is a **host** process competing with the app for CPU. When a
  transition freezes in a take, the app is fine and the *recording* is not — chasing it as a
  main-thread stall finds nothing.
- The stall is probabilistic. Measure each take with `ffmpeg … freezedetect` rather than
  trusting that a fix held.
- The recorder writes a frame only when the screen changes, so a take *ends at the last
  change* — a closing hold has to be rebuilt with `tpad`.
- Finding the launch frame by lightness alone opens the take on the springboard.

## 5. Staged plan

Ordered so that each stage is independently useful and the video tooling comes last.

### Stage 1 — Simulator-runnable capture flows — **done**

`DocumentScannerView` became a plain `View` that picks between the real
`VNDocumentCameraViewController` and a substitute, instead of being the representable itself.
The three call sites are unchanged, and on a device nothing about the behaviour differs.

- `SubstituteScannerView` — the non-camera path, same `(UIImage?) -> Void` contract. Offers
  the photo library in any build, plus the sample images below in DEBUG. It is *not*
  DEBUG-only: a release build on a device whose camera is unsupported now degrades to a
  picker rather than presenting a blank sheet.
- `SampleScanImages` (DEBUG) — four shelf tags and a receipt, drawn with Core Graphics at
  runtime. Nothing binary in the repo, and a new sample is a struct literal.
- `ScannerImageProvider` (DEBUG) — a queue of images handed to the next scanner presentation
  with no UI drawn. **This is the hook Stage 5's director plugs into.** Nothing in the app
  enqueues yet; only the tests do.
- `DocumentScannerView.source(cameraIsSupported:isSimulator:hasQueuedImages:)` — the choice
  itself, pulled out as a pure function so it can be tested. It is the piece that was wrong
  the first time and failed silently, so it is exhaustively covered by
  `GFPriceCheckerTests/DocumentScannerSourceTests.swift`, which also asserts that the test
  bundle's own environment resolves to the substitute.
- `GFPriceCheckerTests/SubstituteScannerTests.swift` — runs the real `OCRService` and
  `ReceiptParser` over the drawn images. This is load-bearing, not decoration: a
  sample the flow cannot read a price off fails *silently*, because the flow still advances
  to the review step, just with every field empty.

**Acceptance:** met. Full suite green, clean Debug and Release builds, no warnings, and the
end-to-end walkthrough verified in the iPhone 17 Pro Simulator — both capture flows run start
to finish with no camera.

Note for anyone repeating that walkthrough: **set Xcode's run destination to a Simulator.** On
a physical device the substitute never appears, by design — the real scanner works there and
is the better path — so a device run looks exactly like the unfixed build and proves nothing.

**A third thing learned, the expensive way:** green tests around a seam say nothing about
whether the seam is *reached*. Everything about the substitute scanner was covered and
passing while the app still dead-ended, because no test touched the dispatch. Any stage here
that adds a branch on the environment should test the branch, not just what is on the far
side of it.

Two things learned that Stages 6-7 will want (see also the comment on `drawRow`):

- **Vision splits a text line at any gap wider than a single space.** A receipt drawn the way
  receipts actually print — label left, amount right-aligned at the margin — comes back with
  every amount as its own observation, so the label's line carries no amount at all. Dot
  leaders make it worse (the dot run becomes a third token); a two-space gap splits
  unpredictably. Only a single space holds. This is a fact about Vision and still true.
- ~~**That is probably a real parser bug.** Not fixed here.~~ **It was, and it is now fixed**
  — issue #51. `extractTotal` required "TOTAL" and a `$`-prefixed amount on the *same* line;
  real receipts print the amount bare and right-aligned into a second column, so it missed
  them on real scans for exactly the reason it missed the drawn one. The `$` is now optional,
  and a total line carrying no amount falls through to the next line when that line is
  nothing but an amount.

**What that means for Stage 6:** the constraint is lifted. `SampleScanImages.receipt` now
takes a `RowStyle` and defaults to `.twoColumn`, so **the receipt the storyboards record is
drawn the way a receipt actually prints**, rather than in the accommodating layout the parser
used to need. The demo videos no longer feature a receipt that is subtly not one. `.inline`
survives as a named style, and both receipt tests run over both layouts.

One thing Stage 6 inherits rather than escapes: the fix reads the amount from the line *after*
the label, which holds only because `extractReceiptText` sorts observations by
`boundingBox.origin.y` and the label's box sorts above its amount's — measured at 19% of a
line height on the fixture. `SubstituteScannerTests.twoColumnLabelSortsAboveItsAmount` pins
it. If a storyboard ever records a receipt at an angle and the total stops prefilling, that
test names the repair: band observations into rows in `extractReceiptText`, **not** a
look-behind, which would read the tax amount on a `SUBTOTAL / HST / TOTAL` receipt.

`Tools/Demo/scrub_backup.py` still draws its substitute receipts single-space. That is now a
free choice rather than a constraint; it is left alone because nothing OCRs those images
(`extractedOCRText` is regenerated from the same lines), so switching them is a purely visual
change that wants an eye on the rendered result.

### Stage 2 — seed data — **done**
- ~~Build a scrubbing step (§3.3).~~ `Tools/Demo/scrub_backup.py`, with
  `Tools/Demo/README.md`. It also verifies its own output, both for PII and for whether
  `importBackup` will read it — the Codable structs are mirrored there as a schema, so it
  catches a date without a timezone, a `version` other than `"1.0"`, an unparseable price
  (which imports silently as free) and a `receiptID` pointing at nothing (which imports the
  product silently unverified).
- ~~A DEBUG seeding entry point that does `clearAllData` then `importBackup(.replace)`.~~
  `GFPriceChecker/Dev/DemoSeed.swift`, driven by `GFPC_DEMO_DATASET`, plus `Tools/Demo/seed.sh`
  as the one command. `.replace` calls `clearAllData` itself, so it is one call, not two.
  `DemoSeedTests` seeds from JSON the scrubber actually produced, which is what keeps the
  Python schema mirror honest.
- ~~Export a real backup from the device.~~ Done. 78 products and 47 of the 48 receipts
  over 2025-12-30 to 2026-09-11, seeded into the Simulator and counted in the store.
- **Acceptance:** `Tools/Demo/seed.sh` puts the Simulator in a known, populated, PII-free
  state — **met**, against the real library.

Three things the first real run turned up that the fixture could not:

- **Size.** The raw library scrubbed to 170 MB, 168 of it shelf-tag photographs — past the
  100 MB a Git host takes for one file, and this dataset is meant to be committed. The
  photographs are now re-encoded at 1600px/q75 on the way through, which brings it to 28 MB.
  That also drops their metadata, which matters more than the bytes: these ones carried
  nothing but dimensions, because `ImageStorageService` had already re-encoded them, but a
  photograph that reached the library another way would still have EXIF, and that carries GPS.
- **A receipt nothing verified** redrew as a blank slip totalling $0.00, because the scrub had
  no items to draw from. Those are now dropped rather than drawn.
- **`seed.sh` computed the built product path** from `-derivedDataPath`, which Xcode's
  Build Location preference overrides when it is set to Custom or to a shared folder. The
  build succeeds and the install then cannot find the app. It asks `-showBuildSettings` now.

### Stage 3 — `CaptureFlowViewModel` — **done**
- ~~Lift `CaptureStep`, the field state and the handler methods out of
  `ProductCaptureFlowView` into an observable ViewModel.~~
  `GFPriceChecker/ViewModels/CaptureFlowViewModel.swift`. `CaptureStep`, `SizeInputMode` and
  `UnitPriceBasis` moved with it; the view is 986 lines down to 516 and now only draws.
- ~~Confirm no behaviour change in the flow on device.~~ Verified on device — the flow runs
  as it did before the extraction.
- **Acceptance:** the flow is driveable by method calls, and its logic is unit-testable —
  **met**. `CaptureFlowViewModelTests` is 32 tests that walk a whole capture without a camera.

The device check was the one that mattered here, and it is worth saying why: the suite covers
a capture driven through `Dependencies`, which is precisely the path a real camera does *not*
take. Nothing green in it would have caught a regression in what VisionKit hands back or in
what the real `OCRService` reads off a shelf tag under store lighting.

Two things worth carrying into Stage 5:

- **The services are behind closures, not protocols.** `CaptureFlowViewModel.Dependencies`
  holds `saveImage`, `detectRectangles` and `extractText`, defaulting to the real
  `ImageStorageService` and `OCRService`. That is what lets a test run a capture end to end
  with canned observations and no Vision framework, and it is also where a demo script
  supplies fixed OCR results instead of driving `ScannerImageProvider` through the scanner.
  Note the init takes `Dependencies?`, not `Dependencies = .live`: a default argument is
  evaluated in the *caller's* isolation, and `.live` is main-actor isolated.
- **The finalize screen no longer re-derives the differential.** It and `save` both went
  through the same unit-price back-calculation, written out twice; it is now
  `gfSizeValue` / `normalizedDifferential` on the view model, so a preview that disagrees with
  what gets saved is no longer possible.

### Stage 4 — addressable navigation — **done**
- ~~`TabView(selection:)` binding, a path binding in the Library, and a way to open the
  capture sheet programmatically.~~ `GFPriceChecker/ViewModels/AppRouter.swift`, an
  `@Observable` in the environment holding the selected tab, the Library's
  `NavigationStack` path (`LibraryRoute`: a product or a receipt, value-based links), and
  each tab's sheet. `show(_ destination:)` puts the app on any of them from anywhere,
  dismissing sheets and emptying the stack first, so the result does not depend on where
  the app was.
- ~~A DEBUG hook.~~ `GFPriceChecker/Dev/DemoRoute.swift`, driven by `GFPC_DEMO_ROUTE`,
  applied after any seed in the same launch. Usage is in `Tools/Demo/README.md`.
- **Acceptance:** a test or DEBUG hook can put the app on any screen without a tap —
  **met**. `AppRouterTests` covers `show(_:)` and the name lookup; every one of the ten
  names was launched against the seeded store and the screen looked at, because the tests
  can only see the router, not whether a view follows it (Stage 1's lesson).

Two things for Stage 5:

- **Each tab has its own sheet slot**, not one shared `Sheet?`. A shared slot would be bound
  by a `.sheet(item:)` on all three tabs at once, and an off-screen tab's binding could clear
  another tab's sheet.
- **Going from one sheet straight to another can be dropped.** `show(_:)` dismisses and
  presents in the same update, and SwiftUI does not reliably present while it is still
  dismissing. A script moving sheet to sheet should `show(.tab(…))` and hold a settle
  interval first, which the director's `async push` gives it anyway. The launch hook never
  meets this, since it routes from a fresh launch.

### Stage 5 — director and curtain — **done**
- ~~Port the Tier-2 shape: env-var bootstrap, registration hooks, done-file, still-file.~~
  `GFPriceChecker/Dev/DemoDirector.swift`, armed by `GFPC_DEMO_SCRIPT` in the App's `init`,
  with `DemoScripts.swift` holding the scripts and `DemoCurtainView.swift` the curtain.
  `.demoRegistered(viewModel)` is on the three sheets with view models, and is `self` in a
  release build. `Tools/Demo/play.sh` is the host half: launch, wait on the done file or the
  still file, report.
- **Acceptance:** one trivial scripted take runs unattended. **Met.** `play.sh tour` seeds,
  plays and reports `done tour` in about 20 s with no hand on it. `DemoDirectorTests` covers
  the files, registration and primitives.

What differs from TrendFit's shape, and why:

- **No navigation hook.** TrendFit's `.demoNavigation` handed a screen's path to the
  director. Here `AppRouter` already is the navigation (Stage 4), so the App gives it to
  the director directly. Registration is the only release hook.
- **The director holds the curtain, not a timer.** The seed runs in the same launch and
  takes longer than 1.5 s, so the curtain lifts `curtainSeconds` after the seed lands.
  The take never shows the store filling.
- **The curtain is flat.** This settles §3.1's caveat with a demo-only `systemBackground`
  curtain, not a per-app constant. It was checked against a real recording:
  TrendFit's `launch_frame.py`, unmodified, cut on the first drawn frame. Numbers are in
  `Tools/Demo/README.md`.
- **A failed script writes the done file**, with the error, and `play.sh` exits non-zero.
  TrendFit logs and finishes as if it had succeeded. That would let Stage 6 cut a half-played
  take.
- **No prewarm view.** TrendFit's `DemoPrewarmView` exists for Swift Charts and MapKit first
  draws. Stage 6's `freezedetect` did find stalls, on a tab's first render, and
  `DemoScripts.warm` covers them by showing the tab once behind the curtain.

For Stage 6: `record.sh` should wrap `play.sh`'s launch rather than repeat it, and must set
light appearance, because the cut detector needs it.

### Stage 6 — host scripts and the real storyboards — **done**
- ~~Port `record.sh` and `launch_frame.py`; write the storyboards for the three videos.~~
  `Tools/Demo/record.sh`, `launch_frame.py` and `stills.sh`, on `lib.sh`'s launch, so a take is
  what `play.sh` plays with a recorder around it. The storyboards are `price-tracking`,
  `receipt-management` and `reporting-exporting` in `DemoScripts.swift`.
- **Acceptance:** `record.sh price-tracking` produces a usable take. **Met**, twice in a row
  (31.3 s and 30.0 s), with `freezedetect` showing only scripted holds on both.

What it turned up, all written up in `Tools/Demo/README.md`:

- **`-r 60` misplaces frames** on the recorder's sparse 600 fps timeline. The fix is the `fps=60`
  filter. TrendFit's `record.sh` carries the same `-r 60`.
- **The flatness caveat stayed settled.** The detector cut on the first drawn frame of every
  take, with its constants unchanged.
- **Stalls:** a tab's first render (warmed behind the curtain), and a Library push made while
  a sheet is still dismissing (given room in the script).
- **An app bug.** The receipt sheet read "Verifying product 0 of 0" as it slid away after a
  save, because saving emptied its query. `ReceiptCaptureViewModel` now stops following the
  query once it has saved.
- **Question 3 answered (stills):** yes. `stills.sh` cuts the three carousel screenshots
  whose screens the videos show, from the same scripts.

### Stage 7 — compositing
- The stage TrendFit does not have: portrait capture → 1920×1080 frame, burned-in captions
  from a timed list living next to the storyboard, audio track.
- **Acceptance:** output matches the current site videos' spec, regenerable from a command.

## 6. Open questions

1. **Scope of the media set.** Regenerate the existing three videos only, or take the chance
   to restructure what the site teaches?
2. **Audio.** Re-record narration per build is the expensive part and does not script. Options:
   keep narration but re-use the audio track across builds where the script is unchanged; move
   to captions only; or generate narration.
3. ~~**Do the stills matter here?**~~ **Answered in Stage 6:** three of the carousel's
   screenshots show the screens the videos do, and `stills.sh` cuts them from the same scripts.
4. ~~**Where do demo assets live?**~~ **Answered, and revised once the real export existed:**
   the raw backup lives in `~/GFPCDemoData/`, outside the repo, with `.gitignore` covering
   `backup-raw.json` and `GFPCDemoData/` in case a copy lands inside. The **scrubbed dataset
   is committed**, at `Tools/Demo/demo-dataset.json`. Keeping it out was the original answer
   and it was wrong: the raw backup must never be committed, so a dataset that exists only in
   `~/GFPCDemoData/` exists on one Mac, and losing that Mac means re-shooting the library —
   which is the exact problem this pipeline is for. 28 MB is the price of the guarantee.
